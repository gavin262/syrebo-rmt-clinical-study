Robot Glove Plus Mirror Therapy: How Should We Read the SY-HR06 Three-Arm Pilot Study?

Qian et al. · Frontiers in Neurology · Published 16 June 2025

ORIGINAL PAPER · OPEN ACCESS UNDER CC BY

Combination of robot-assisted glove and mirror therapy improves upper limb motor function in subacute stroke patients: a randomized controlled pilot study

Qian J, Liang C, Liu R, Yu J, Yang T, Bai D

Frontiers in Neurology, Volume 16, 1602896 · DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1602896

Wuhan Wuchang Hospital · ChiCTR2200057613 · Read the original paper

This was not simply a comparison of “glove versus no glove.” The investigators studied mirror therapy, robot-assisted glove therapy, and their combination in three parallel groups. Its main value is an early clinical signal for the combined pathway. Its main risk of misinterpretation is attributing every improvement to the glove alone.

Quick answer: Among 52 participants who completed and were analyzed, four weeks of Yisheng SY-HR06 robot-glove training combined with mirror therapy showed promising results. Post-treatment FMA-UE was higher in the combined group than in the mirror-therapy group (37.61±11.09 vs 23.63±12.69; p=0.006). This was a pilot study without follow-up, not proof of sustained efficacy.

Study design: 66 randomized, 52 completed and analyzed

Conducted at Wuhan Wuchang Hospital, the study used a single-blind, randomized, controlled design. Of 100 people screened, 66 were eligible and randomized. Fourteen withdrew during the intervention and were excluded from the data analysis. The final analysis therefore included 16 in MT, 18 in RT, and 18 in RMT—52 participants in total. The accurate description is “66 randomized, 52 completed and analyzed,” not “52 randomized.”

All groups received usual rehabilitation. Outcomes included FMA-UE, Brunnstrom upper-limb and hand stages, FTHUE-HK, and the Functional Independence Measure (FIM). A certified occupational therapist blinded to group allocation conducted the assessments.

Key results: the combined group outperformed mirror therapy on selected outcomes

Several outcomes improved from baseline in all three groups. For between-group interpretation, post-treatment FMA-UE was 37.61±11.09 in RMT and 23.63±12.69 in MT; the paper reports p=0.006 for this comparison. In RMT, post-treatment Brunnstrom upper-limb stage was 4.06±0.87, hand stage was 4.67±1.24, and FIM was 94.17±9.49. The abstract reports statistically significant advantages for these outcomes compared with MT.

These findings do not establish that the glove alone was superior to every alternative. RMT combined two interventions. The abstract's clearest comparisons are RMT versus MT, and not every outcome establishes a statistically significant difference between RMT and RT. The appropriate clinical reading is that the combined approach produced a promising signal in this pilot study.

What does this add to clinical discussions?

The study placed visual feedback and repeated affected-hand movement within the same training session, while presenting the combined approach alongside each component. For hospitals and rehabilitation providers, this supports discussion of a therapist-directed treatment pathway: usual rehabilitation remains the foundation, with mirror therapy, robot-glove training, or their combination selected according to the patient. It does not position the device as a replacement for clinicians or conventional rehabilitation.

Limitations that must accompany the results

  • Pilot scale: only 16–18 participants per group were analyzed; larger studies are needed.
  • Four weeks, no follow-up: sustained long-term effects remain unknown.
  • Fourteen withdrawals were excluded: findings are based on the 52 completers, which matters when interpreting the analysis.
  • Lesion-side distribution differed: the authors note a higher proportion of right-hemisphere lesions in RMT, which may affect recovery.
  • Active participation was not quantified: actual grasp counts were not compared, so differences in training engagement cannot be ruled out.
Responsible wording: “A three-arm randomized controlled pilot study evaluated Yisheng SY-HR06 robot-glove training combined with mirror therapy and provided an early clinical signal for this combined approach.” Avoid “proven long-term efficacy,” “suitable for every stroke patient,” or “all improvement was caused by the glove alone.”
